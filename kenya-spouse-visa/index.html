Kenya Spouse Visa

A deep dive into residency when you marry a Kenyan citizen. Understand what is required, how to prove your marriage, and how to navigate the process without costly delays.

What is required Read the FAQs

The short answer

Marrying a Kenyan citizen opens a route to residency that is independent of an employer or a business. However, it is not automatic. You must apply for a residence permit, prove that the marriage is genuine and ongoing, and provide a file that meets strict documentary standards. The application is assessed on its facts; simply holding a marriage certificate is rarely enough on its own.

1. How the route works

For many couples, marriage provides a level of stability that employment or investment routes cannot. Your right to remain in Kenya is tied to your relationship, not to a job contract or a company bank balance. If your circumstances change, your right to stay does not automatically disappear with them.

However, the authorities are careful about marriages entered into solely for immigration purposes. The entire application is built around proving two things: that the marriage is legally valid, and that it represents a genuine, ongoing relationship.

2. Core requirements

While the exact checklist can vary slightly depending on your country of origin and current regulations, the foundational documents remain consistent:

  • Valid Passport: With sufficient validity remaining.
  • Marriage Certificate: The original or certified copy.
  • Proof of Kenyan Citizenship: A copy of your spouse's national ID or passport.
  • Passport Photographs: To the specific specification required.
  • Certificate of Good Conduct: A police clearance from your country of residence.
  • Medical Evidence: Often required to confirm you do not pose a public health risk.
  • Financial Evidence: Proof that the Kenyan spouse can support you, or that you have independent means.

3. Proving a genuine marriage

This is where most files succeed or fail. A marriage certificate proves a legal event took place, but it does not prove a shared life. You need to build a narrative of an ongoing relationship. The strongest evidence usually includes:

  • Shared Finances: Joint bank accounts, transfers between your accounts over time, or shared financial obligations.
  • Shared Residence: A lease agreement in both names, utility bills, or official correspondence sent to the same address.
  • Photographic Evidence: Photos of the two of you together over a span of time, ideally with family or on significant occasions, not just a single holiday.
  • Communication Records: Call logs, chat histories, or emails spanning the duration of your relationship.
  • Affidavits: Sworn statements from family members, friends, or community leaders who can attest to the genuineness of the marriage.
  • Travel Records: Stamps in your passports showing visits to each other before the marriage.

Consistency is crucial. If your application says you met in 2021, but a photo is dated 2019, or a name is spelled differently on the marriage certificate versus the passport, explain it. Do not leave the assessor to guess.

4. Marrying outside Kenya

If you married outside Kenya, the process adds a layer of administration. Your marriage certificate must be recognised by the Kenyan authorities. This usually involves:

  • Legalisation: Depending on the country that issued the certificate, you may need an apostille or consular legalisation.
  • Translation: If the certificate is not in English, a certified translation by an approved translator is mandatory.

This is frequently the slowest step in the entire process. Do not wait until you are ready to submit the permit application to start the legalisation process; begin it immediately after the wedding.

5. Working on a spouse permit

Historically, a spouse permit allowed the holder to live and work in Kenya. However, immigration rules and permit conditions change. It is essential to confirm the exact privileges attached to your specific permit at the time of issuance.

If you intend to run a local business, take up employment with a Kenyan company, or engage in professional work, you should ensure your permit explicitly covers this, or you may need to apply for a separate Class D (Employment) or Class G (Investor) permit alongside it.

6. Common pitfalls

  • Submitting uncertified copies: The authorities often want originals or copies certified by a commissioner of oaths.
  • Name discrepancies: A missing middle name, a maiden name not updated, or a slightly different spelling across documents can cause weeks of delay.
  • Assuming approval: Strong evidence helps, but no application is guaranteed. Plan your move around your own timeline, not an assumed approval date.
  • Ignoring the marriage's age: A very recent marriage with no shared history or financial entanglement will face higher scrutiny. You will need to work harder to prove the relationship is real.

8. Frequently asked questions

Does marrying a Kenyan citizen automatically grant me residency?

No. While marriage provides a route to residency that is independent of an employer or business, it is not automatic. You must still apply for the appropriate residence permit, provide a substantial file of evidence proving the marriage is genuine, and wait for the authorities to assess and approve the application.

What is the difference between a spouse visa and a dependant pass?

A dependant pass is typically issued to the spouse or children of a foreign national who holds a work or business permit in Kenya. A spouse visa or residence permit applies specifically to the foreign spouse of a Kenyan citizen, giving them an independent right to reside in the country.

How do I prove my marriage is genuine for the application?

You need to demonstrate an ongoing, genuine relationship, not just a marriage certificate. This usually includes joint bank accounts or financial transfers, a shared lease or property ownership, photographs together over a period of time, travel records, correspondence, and affidavits from family or friends who know you both.

What if we got married outside of Kenya?

If your marriage certificate was issued outside Kenya, it must usually be legalised (often via an apostille, depending on the issuing country) to be recognised. If the certificate is not in English, a certified translation is also required. This is frequently the slowest part of the application.

Can I work in Kenya on a spouse visa?

The rights attached to a spouse residence permit vary, but generally, they allow you to live in Kenya. If you intend to work for a Kenyan employer or run a local business, you may still need a separate work or business permit, or a special pass depending on your specific activities. You should confirm the exact conditions of your permit upon issuance.

How long does it take to get a spouse permit?

Timelines vary based on the completeness of your file and current processing volumes. Files that lack legalised documents or clear proof of a shared life often face delays or queries. It is best to plan for several months and avoid booking irreversible travel or shipping until you have clarity.

Do we need to be married for a certain amount of time before applying?

There is no strict minimum period for the permit route, but a very recent marriage with little documentary evidence of shared life may face higher scrutiny. The authorities assess the genuineness of the relationship, so the more established the marriage is in terms of shared finances and residence, the stronger the file.

Can my children from a previous relationship be included?

This depends on the specific category and custody arrangements. You will typically need to provide birth certificates, legal custody documents, and consent from the other biological parent. It is a complex area of immigration and should be discussed with a legal advisor before submitting the application.

What happens to my residency if we divorce or separate?

A permit based on marriage is tied to that marriage. If the marriage ends, your right to reside under that specific permit may be affected, and you may need to apply for a different status or leave the country. You should seek legal advice immediately if your circumstances change.

Do you guarantee my spouse visa will be approved?

No. Every application is assessed on its own facts by the authorities, and no one can guarantee an outcome. What we can do is help you assemble a consistent, well-documented file that anticipates queries and meets the required standards.

Confirm current requirements and take qualified advice on your specific circumstances before you apply or commit to travel.
