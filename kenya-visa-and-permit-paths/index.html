Kenya Visa and Permit Paths

Every route into Kenya, explained in one place: visitor and entry authorisations, the digital nomad route, student passes, work permits, investor and business permits, residence, dependant passes and permanent residence.

Compare the routes Read the FAQs

The short answer

Kenya separates entry from stay. An entry authorisation lets you travel here for a short visit. A permit lets you remain for a longer period for a specific purpose — work, business, investment, study, residence, or as the dependant of someone who holds a permit. Almost everyone who moves to Kenya needs both, in that order. Choosing the correct permit category before you apply is the single decision that most affects your timeline.

1. How the system fits together

Three terms are used constantly and often mixed up. Getting them straight makes the rest of this page much easier to follow.

  • Entry authorisation (visa). Permission to travel to Kenya and remain for a short period. It covers tourism, short business meetings, transit and family visits. It does not carry the right to work or run a business.
  • Permit. Permission to remain for a longer period for a defined purpose — employment, business, investment, residence or study. A permit is what turns a visit into a life.
  • Pass. A specific permission tied to a person or a purpose. Dependant passes attach to a permit holder. Student passes attach to an institution. Special passes cover short-term activity or bridge a gap while a longer application is processed.

Because permits depend on a purpose, the first question is never "which permit is quickest" but "what will you actually be doing here". A founder running a company, an employee transferring with an employer, a remote worker paid from abroad and a spouse of a Kenyan citizen are four completely different files.

2. Visitor and entry authorisations

Most people arrive in Kenya on an entry authorisation. It is the front door, and for many it is enough — a holiday, a scouting trip, a conference, a family visit.

What it is for

  • Tourism, safaris, beach stays and short family visits.
  • Short business trips: meetings, conferences, negotiations, site visits.
  • Transit through a Kenyan airport.
  • Scouting a move before committing to a permit.

What it does not do

An entry authorisation does not permit employment, running a business or earning income locally. It also does not convert automatically into a permit. Working on a visitor entry can complicate later applications, and it is not worth the risk.

Variants worth knowing

Single-entry authorisations suit one trip. Multiple-entry authorisations suit people who will move in and out during a project or a relocation. East African residents and citizens have separate regional arrangements. Nationality determines whether you need to apply in advance or travel visa-free for a short stay — and that list changes, so confirm your position before booking flights, because airlines check it at check-in.

Using a visit to plan a move

A short visit is one of the most useful tools in a relocation. It lets you see neighbourhoods at rush hour, visit schools, meet lawyers and confirm costs. If you plan to do this, say so at the border if asked, keep your itinerary simple, and do not carry a container of belongings.

3. The digital nomad route

Kenya introduced a route for people who live here but are paid elsewhere — the remote worker or digital nomad category. It recognises a reality that the older system did not: many people now earn from abroad while spending their lives in one place.

Who it suits

  • Remote employees of overseas companies.
  • Freelancers and consultants with international clients.
  • Founders of businesses that operate entirely outside Kenya.
  • People testing whether Nairobi or the coast suits them before committing to something longer.

What applications generally require

The core of the file is proof that your income is genuinely foreign and stable. Expect to provide evidence of remote employment or client contracts, bank statements showing regular income above a set threshold, valid health insurance covering Kenya, a certificate of good conduct, and a passport with sufficient validity. Some applicants also provide a local address and details of how they will be supported administratively while in the country.

The income threshold, validity period and fee are set by the authorities and are adjusted from time to time. Confirm the current figures before you build a budget around them.

The limits

This route is for people whose income comes from outside Kenya. It does not authorise local employment or running a Kenyan business that earns locally — if that is the plan, a business or investor permit is the right route. It also does not lead directly to permanent residence.

4. Student routes

Kenya hosts a substantial international student population, from language and vocational courses to full degree programmes at public and private universities.

How it works

The student route is tied to an institution. You need an offer or admission letter, evidence of fees paid or funded, proof of maintenance, health cover and a certificate of good conduct. The institution usually provides guidance on the process, but the responsibility for a complete file remains with the student.

Practical points

  • Confirm the institution's registration and the specific programme before paying any fees.
  • Apply well ahead of the academic term; student files are seasonal and volumes peak.
  • Check whether your family can accompany you on dependant passes — often limited for students.
  • Keep attendance and progress records. Status is tied to active study, and gaps can affect renewals.

5. Employment and work permits

This is the route most people picture when they think of relocating for a job. It is also the route that requires the most coordination between you and a Kenyan employer.

Who it suits

  • People taking up a role with a Kenyan employer.
  • Employees transferring within an international organisation.
  • Specialist roles where local skills are not readily available.
  • Volunteers and staff of international organisations, where a specific arrangement applies.

What the file usually contains

  • An employment contract or formal offer letter.
  • The employer's registration and tax records.
  • Evidence that the position was advertised locally, where required.
  • Your qualifications, CV and professional references.
  • Passport, photographs, certificate of good conduct and medical evidence.

Some categories are assessed on whether the role could be filled locally. That test is the reason a strong file explains not just who you are, but why the role needs you specifically.

Timing

Employer-side documents take longer than most candidates expect, particularly company registration records and tax compliance certificates. Start those conversations on day one. If you have a fixed start date, treat it as a deadline to work backwards from — not a date to hope for.

6. Investor and business permits

Kenya actively welcomes people who build companies here, and this category reflects that. It suits founders, directors and investors who intend to operate a real commercial presence.

The order that usually works

  1. Register the company — reserve the name, incorporate, appoint directors and a company secretary, and secure a registered address.
  2. Obtain tax registration for the entity and for yourself.
  3. Open a corporate bank account — this often takes longer than expected.
  4. Build substance — premises, staff, contracts, capital actually deployed.
  5. Apply for the permit that matches your role in the business.

What investors usually need to evidence

Investment routes turn on demonstrating that capital has genuinely entered the country and is committed to the venture. Expect to show the source of funds, the transfer trail into a Kenyan account, and a business plan explaining what the money will do. Capital that sits untouched, with no operational activity around it, weakens a file considerably.

Sector checks come first

Certain sectors carry additional licensing requirements, and some activities are restricted or reserved. A licence you cannot obtain changes the entire structure, so check sector rules before finalising shareholding.

Owning a company is not the same as living here

A registered company does not by itself give you the right to reside. Your status depends on your role — whether you are a director running the business day to day, an investor whose capital underpins it, or an employee of the local entity. The same person can sometimes qualify under more than one heading.

7. Residence permits

Residence routes are for people with a longer-term connection to Kenya that is not primarily an employer relationship.

Who it suits

  • Spouses of Kenyan citizens.
  • People with substantial, long-standing investment in the country.
  • Long-term residents with an established history.
  • Retirees and people of independent means, where the category allows.

Marriage-based residence

Marriage opens a route that does not depend on an employer, which is valuable. It is not automatic. The certificate usually needs legalisation and translation, and the file benefits from evidence of an ongoing marriage: shared residence, financial links, correspondence over time, and consistent accounts of how you met. Where two documents conflict on a date or a spelling, explain it rather than leaving an assessor to notice it.

Investment-based residence

Where the qualifying basis is capital rather than family, the evidential burden shifts to source of funds, transfer records and the economic contribution of the investment. Longevity matters: a file showing years of consistent activity is stronger than one showing a single large transfer.

8. Dependant passes

When one person moves for work, business or study, the rest of the family needs their own permission to stay. That is what a dependant pass provides.

The one rule to remember

A dependant pass does not exist independently. It hangs off the main permit. If the main permit lapses or is withdrawn, the dependant passes attached to it are affected. This shapes the entire sequence of a family relocation.

Who is usually covered

Spouses and dependent children in the typical case. Some routes also allow other dependent relatives where the relationship and dependency can be evidenced. Children approaching the age at which they stop being treated as dependants need particular attention — plan a student pass or another status rather than assuming the dependant pass will carry them.

Documents that carry the most weight

  • Marriage certificate — legalised where required, translated if not in English.
  • Birth certificates for each child, with the same checks.
  • Passports with validity covering the intended period.
  • The principal applicant's permit, or evidence it is being processed.
  • Photographs to the stated specification.
  • Evidence of financial support where requested.

Where parents are separated, custody documentation and consent from the other parent are frequently requested. Where a name has changed, include the chain of evidence rather than explaining it in a covering letter.

9. Special passes

A special pass is a short-term permission for a specific activity, or a bridge while a longer application is in progress. It is one of the most useful tools in the system and one of the most misunderstood.

  • It can allow you to remain lawfully while a permit is being decided.
  • It can cover a defined short assignment, research trip or contractual engagement.
  • It is time-limited and is not a substitute for the permit you actually need.

Because the rules differ by category, whether a special pass is available to you — and for how long — should be confirmed for your specific route before you travel.

10. Permanent residence

Permanent residence is a separate, higher-threshold route for people with a long and established connection to Kenya. It is assessed on factors such as years of lawful residence, investment, family ties and other qualifying grounds.

It is not a natural next step after a first permit, and it should not be assumed. It is usually planned years in advance, with qualified advice, and the file is judged on the whole history — including how consistently you have complied with earlier permits and renewals.

13. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a Kenyan visa and a Kenyan permit?

A visa or entry authorisation allows you to travel to Kenya and stay for a short period, typically for tourism, business meetings, transit or a family visit. A permit allows you to remain for a longer period for a defined purpose, such as employment, business, investment, study, residence or as the dependant of a permit holder. Most people who move to Kenya need an entry authorisation first and a permit afterwards.

Do I need a visa before travelling to Kenya?

Most visitors need an entry authorisation obtained before travel, though the exact requirement depends on nationality and can change. Some nationalities travel visa-free for short visits. Confirm your specific position before booking flights, because airlines check your authorisation at check-in.

Can I work in Kenya on a visitor visa?

No. A visitor authorisation does not give you the right to work, run a business or earn locally. Working on a visitor entry can affect future applications. If you intend to work, you need the appropriate work, business or investor permit, and in many cases a special pass while that application is being processed.

What is the Kenya digital nomad visa?

Kenya introduced a route for remote workers who are employed by, or earn from, organisations outside the country. It is designed for people who live in Kenya but whose income comes from abroad. Applications generally require proof of remote employment or contracts, evidence of income above a set threshold, valid health insurance, a clean conduct record and a passport with sufficient validity. The income threshold and fee are set by the authorities and should be confirmed before applying.

Can my spouse and children move with me?

Usually yes. Spouses and dependent children can apply for dependant passes linked to the main permit holder. Dependant passes do not exist independently: if the main permit lapses or is withdrawn, the dependant passes attached to it are affected. Relationship evidence such as marriage and birth certificates, legalised and translated where necessary, is central to the application.

How long does a Kenyan visa or permit take to process?

Timelines vary widely by category, by the completeness of the file and by current volumes. Some routes move in a few weeks, others take considerably longer. Rather than planning around a best case, work backwards from your own deadline — a job start date, a school term — and allow time for queries and requests for further documents.

Can I apply for a permit from inside Kenya?

In many cases applications can be lodged while you are in the country, but your ability to remain while it is processed depends on the status you hold. A special pass is sometimes used to bridge the gap. The rules differ by category, so confirm this for your specific route before you travel.

What documents are usually required?

Most applications ask for a passport with sufficient validity, completed forms, passport photographs, a certificate of good conduct, medical evidence, and financial information. Work and business routes add employer letters, contracts, company records or proof of investment. Family routes add marriage and birth certificates. Documents issued abroad often need legalisation and, where not in English, certified translation.

Do you guarantee that my visa or permit will be approved?

No. Every application is assessed on its own facts by the authorities, and no one can guarantee an outcome. What we can do is help you choose the right route, assemble a consistent file, anticipate queries and avoid the errors that most often cause delay or refusal.

Can I switch from a visitor entry to a work or business permit?

It is sometimes possible, but it is not automatic and depends on the category and your current status. Switching after arrival can extend your overall timeline, and some routes are better started from your home country. If you know you intend to work or run a business in Kenya, it is usually better to plan the correct route from the outset.

How long is a Kenyan permit valid, and can it be renewed?

Validity depends on the category, commonly running for one to two years for many work and business permits. Renewals are usually possible where the underlying purpose still exists and the compliance history is clean. Dependant passes are renewed in step with the main permit, so both sets of expiry dates should be tracked together.

Can I apply for permanent residence in Kenya?

Permanent residence is a separate, higher-threshold route intended for people with a long and established connection to Kenya. It is assessed on years of lawful residence, investment, family ties and other qualifying factors. It is not a natural next step after a first permit, and it should be planned well in advance with qualified advice.

Which countries do you help people move from?

We have helped people relocating from Italy, Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom and many other countries across Europe, North America, the Middle East, Africa and Asia. Document requirements are broadly similar, but legalisation and translation requirements differ by country, and that is where local knowledge saves the most time.
